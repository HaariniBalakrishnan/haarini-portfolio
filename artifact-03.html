01 / INTRODUCTION & OBJECTIVE

Relationships are valuable. Attention is limited.

At Arches, account priorities were guided by management. Activation accounts—firms with existing global contracts—were the ongoing priority, while I also developed relationships across other assigned accounts. When a manager planned a U.S. visit, our outreach shifted toward accounts in the destination city. This independent portfolio prototype explores how those business rules could be made visible and supplemented with explainable scoring; it does not reproduce an internal Arches system.

Objective: Create a fictional, interactive account-prioritization tool that distinguishes ongoing activation priorities from geography-driven visit planning, then uses adjustable scores to compare accounts within the relevant group.

03 / PROCESS

From business problem to prototype.

STEP 01

Define the decision

Map two business situations: ongoing activation-account priority and temporary geographic focus when management visits the U.S.

STEP 02

Select features

Represent activation status, office location, relationship readiness, industry fit, engagement, and opportunity potential using fictional profiles.

STEP 03

Create synthetic data

Create six invented consulting accounts across Chicago, New York, Boston, California, and an additional city, without naming real clients or using CRM data.

STEP 04

Build and evaluate

Implement business-rule ranking, destination filtering, adjustable weighted scores, explanations, and scenario checks.

04 / METHODS & TOOLS

Make the logic visible.

The model has two layers. First, business rules determine which accounts are eligible and their priority group. In standard mode, activation accounts come first. In visit mode, accounts in the selected destination come first; an “Other” option illustrates relationship-driven exceptions. Second, a weighted average ranks accounts within the same group. Sliders are normalized to keep scores on a 0–100 scale.

Priority logic: Business rules determine the groups; numerical scores break ties and rank within each group. A high score cannot move a non-activation account ahead of an activation account in standard mode.

Scoring formula
Score = (fit × fit weight + engagement × engagement weight + potential × potential weight) ÷ sum of weights

05 / EVALUATION & LEARNING

Test the assumptions, not just the interface.

The dashboard offers two operating modes and two weighting presets. Standard mode always prioritizes activation accounts; visit mode focuses on the selected geography. Relationship-first and growth-first presets change the order within a priority group, allowing visitors to examine sensitivity to assumptions.Relationship-first increases the influence of existing engagement; Growth-first emphasizes opportunity potential. Comparing the resulting rankings demonstrates how priorities depend on assumptions rather than being objective facts.

  • Changing a weight recalculates every visible account using the same formula.
  • The destination filter changes which accounts appear in the visit-planning shortlist; a location does not change an account’s underlying score.
  • When all three weights are zero, the dashboard prompts the user to choose at least one factor rather than producing misleading results.
  • Each recommendation displays the feature values and contribution to the total score for auditability.

These are functional checks and interpretive exercises, not evidence that the tool improves real-world sales performance. A real deployment would require validated data, stakeholder input, performance monitoring, and privacy review.

06 / VALUE PROPOSITION & PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE

Connect human judgment with structured analysis.

The value of this prototype is not automating relationships. It is helping a user compare accounts consistently, challenge assumptions, and explain prioritization choices. For consulting, research, recruiting, or business operations roles, the project demonstrates how I can translate an ambiguous business problem into a practical, transparent digital tool.

Unlike my earlier chatbot artifact, this project emphasizes structured data, feature engineering, algorithmic decision-making, and sensitivity analysis—foundational ideas that also inform machine-learning workflows.

07 / RESPONSIBLE USE & NEXT STEPS

A decision aid, not a decision-maker.

Weighted scores can amplify biased assumptions or incomplete information. A high score should never override client context, relationship quality, consent, or professional judgment. This educational version uses fictional data and intentionally avoids real client names or private CRM records.

A future iteration could gather consented, anonymized historical outcomes; evaluate whether the selected features are informative; compare the transparent baseline with a trained model; and review fairness, calibration, and accuracy before any real use.
